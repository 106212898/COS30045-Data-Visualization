Television energy consumption

The television is often the centrepiece of the Australian living room — and one of the more energy-intensive entertainment appliances. This page looks at how screen technology and size affect running costs.

Panel technology matters

Placeholder content: different television panel types draw power in different ways. LED-backlit LCD panels are generally the most efficient for everyday brightness levels, while OLED panels vary more with content, since each pixel produces its own light. Older plasma technology, largely phased out of the market, used noticeably more power for a similar screen size.

Screen size, picture mode, and brightness settings all influence real-world consumption, so two televisions with the same Energy Rating star count can still use different amounts of power depending on how they're used at home.

Placeholder tip

Reading the label

The Energy Rating label on a television shows an estimated annual energy use figure in kWh, calculated under standard test conditions — useful for comparing models side by side before you buy.
